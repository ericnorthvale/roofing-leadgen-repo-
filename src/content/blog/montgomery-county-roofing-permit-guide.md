"Do I need a permit to replace my roof?" sounds like it should have one answer.
In south Montgomery County it has at least four, and they change at boundaries
you can't see from the street. Your neighbor across FM 2978 might need nothing;
you might need a covenant approval; the house two exits down might need a full
city permit with inspections. Here's the actual map, jurisdiction by
jurisdiction, sourced from the agencies themselves.

## Unincorporated Montgomery County: no permit

If your home sits in unincorporated Montgomery County — outside any city and
outside The Woodlands Township's covenants — the county itself does not permit
residential re-roofs. The county's own permitting FAQ says it verbatim:
"Residential properties in Montgomery County do not require a permit. However,
it's always a good idea to check with your HOA or POA…"
([Montgomery County Environmental Health permitting FAQ](https://www.mctx.org/departments/departments_d_-_f/environmental_health/permitting/faq.php)).

That second sentence is doing real work. No *county* permit doesn't mean no
approval — most subdivisions out here have an HOA or POA with its own
architectural rules, and the biggest "HOA" of all is next.

## The Woodlands: no county permit, but covenant approval is mandatory

The Woodlands is unincorporated, so the county rule above applies — but the
Township's recorded covenants are binding on every home, and the 2024
Residential Development Standards (recorded December 10, 2024) require prior
written approval for **all roof replacement** (§1.5(I)(A)). Even minor repairs
get no free pass: the minor-repair exemption specifically carves out
"fencing, roofing and color changes, which require a permit at the time of
repair or replacement" (§1.5(I)(B)).

The approval comes from your village's **Residential Design Review Committee
(RDRC)**, elected resident committees staffed by the Township's Covenant
Administration Department. The essentials:

- **Apply through the Civic Access Portal** — the Township's online permitting
  system.
- **No property survey required** for re-roof applications (§1.5(II)(E)),
  though product samples may be requested (§1.5(II)(D)).
- **Materials** (§1.6(H)(4)): tile, slate, composition shingles, stone-coated
  or standing-seam metal, and cement-fiberboard are acceptable. Colors must
  harmonize with the natural landscape — "muted in shades and without color
  pattern" — and metal must be free of glossy finish.
- **The 45-day clock**: the committee has 45 days to act. No action within 45
  days equals **automatic disapproval** (§1.5(III)(A),(B)(4)) — so an
  incomplete application doesn't quietly slide through; it quietly dies.
- **Three approval paths**: full Committee review, faster Staff approval for
  straightforward applications, or Pre-Approval. Some items need no
  application at all — gutters, downspouts, and roof stacks or vents in a
  color compatible with the home are pre-approved (§1.6).
- **Appeals** go to the Development Standards Committee in writing within 10
  days (§1.5(III)(C)).

One genuine oddity: homes in **Grogan's Forest Sections 5, 6 and 7** submit
roof applications to the **City of Shenandoah**, not the Township, per the
Township's own permitting-process page. If that's your section, read on.

## Shenandoah: a full municipal permit program

Cross into the City of Shenandoah and you're in a real permitting regime. The
city's Community Development Department (permitting through
MyGovernmentOnline, built on the 2021 I-Codes) publishes residential roofing
rules that include:

- Shingles installed to a **110-mph, 3-second-gust standard with a 6-nail
  pattern**
- **ASTM D3161 Class F or G** rated shingles, with a product cut sheet
  required at application
- **Earth-tone colors only**
- **Two inspections** — decking and finished shingles
- Contractors must carry **$1M general liability insurance with the City
  listed as certificate holder**

([Shenandoah Residential Roofing Rules and Regulations](https://shenandoahtx.us/DocumentCenter/View/185/Residential-Roofing-Rules-and-Regulations-PDF)).
That certificate-holder requirement quietly filters out under-insured crews —
one reason a Shenandoah re-roof by a fly-by-night operator tends to end at the
permit counter.

## Oak Ridge North: its own building department

Oak Ridge North runs its own Building Department
(permits@oakridgenorth.com, 832-381-3301), with roughly a 10-day plan review
when drawings are required
([Oak Ridge North permits page](https://www.oakridgenorth.com/departments/building_department/permits/index.php)).
If you're inside city limits, start there before scheduling a tear-off.

## Creekside Park: the Harris County question

Creekside Park is the one Woodlands village in Harris County, and here we'll
tell you what we *don't* know: Harris County's unincorporated permits FAQ
centers on floodplain "development" and doesn't list residential re-roof
permits either way. Rather than guess, call the Harris County Office of the
County Engineer permits line at 713-274-3920 for your address. What is certain:
the Township's RDRC covenant approval applies in
[Creekside Park](/the-woodlands/creekside-park) exactly as it does in the
Montgomery County villages.

## The contrast case: City of Houston

Worth stating because so much online advice assumes it: inside Houston city
limits, a re-roof permit **is** required
([Houston Permitting Center](https://www.houstonpermittingcenter.org/hpwcode1140)).
If you've moved up from the city, don't map that experience onto Montgomery
County — the rules here are covenant-driven, not city-hall-driven.

## What still governs the work when there's no permit

No permit desk doesn't mean no standard. Texas Local Government Code
§233.153 ties new residential construction in participating unincorporated
counties to the International Residential Code (re-roofs sit outside that
subchapter's scope, per §233.151) — and the 2021 IRC is what competent roofers
build to regardless. A few citables we follow on every job: **R908.3** requires
roof replacement to be a tear-off to the deck (no roof-overs on water-soaked
decking or where two layers already exist, R908.3.1.1); **R905.2.8.5** requires
drip edge at eaves and rakes; **R903.2** governs flashing; **R806** sets attic
ventilation ratios.

One non-issue to clear up: TDI's windstorm (TWIA) building code and WPI-8
inspections apply only to fourteen coastal counties plus Harris County east of
Highway 146 — **not** Montgomery County, and not Creekside Park. If someone
tries to sell you a "required windstorm certificate" here, that's a tell.

## The quick reference

| Where you live | What you need |
|---|---|
| Unincorporated Montgomery Co. | No county permit; check your HOA/POA |
| The Woodlands (any village) | Township RDRC covenant approval, Civic Access Portal |
| Grogan's Forest Sec. 5–7 | Roof application to City of Shenandoah |
| Shenandoah | Full city permit + inspections |
| Oak Ridge North | City Building Department permit |
| Creekside Park (Harris Co.) | RDRC approval; confirm county status at 713-274-3920 |
