Nobody budgets for a roof the way they budget for a kitchen. It's not fun money —
it's a big check for something you mostly can't see from the driveway. So let's
do this the honest way: real published numbers with their sources attached, the
factors that move your specific price up or down, and what's different about
replacing a roof in The Woodlands.

One thing up front: the figures below are national market data, not Northvale
quotes. Your roof gets its own number, in writing, before we leave.

## The published numbers

**According to Angi (2026),** the national average roof replacement runs
**$9,602**, with a full span of **$5,900 to $46,000** and a typical
**$4–$11 per square foot** installed. That span looks absurd until you remember
it covers everything from a small ranch with basic shingles to a large, steep,
complex roof in premium material.

**This Old House (2026)** pegs the average at about **$15,439 for a 2,000-square-foot
asphalt roof**, and reports that 52% of surveyed homeowners paid between $5,001
and $20,000. Given the size of typical homes in villages like
[Alden Bridge](/the-woodlands/alden-bridge) and Sterling Ridge, that
mid-five-figure zone is a realistic mental anchor for a lot of The Woodlands.

**Homewyse (May 2026)** lands in the same territory, listing asphalt shingle
installation from roughly **$5.09–$6.66 per square foot**. When three
independent sources converge like this, you can trust the shape of the range
even before anyone measures your roof.

**Per square foot by shingle grade, according to Modernize (2026):**

| Shingle type          | Cost per sq ft |
| --------------------- | -------------- |
| 3-tab                 | $3.43–$4.65    |
| Architectural         | $4.11–$5.57    |
| Premium architectural | $4.39–$5.95    |

**Metal is a different bracket.** The Metal Roofing Alliance puts metal at
roughly 2–3× the cost of asphalt — in exchange for a 30–50+ year lifespan.
According to Angi (2026), a standing-seam metal roof averages **$19,000**, with a
range of $9,400–$32,600 and $4–$30 per square foot.

**For contrast:** Angi (2026) reports typical roof _repairs_ run **$400–$2,000**.
If your roof is mid-life with one problem area, [a repair](/services/roof-repair)
may be the right call — a good contractor should tell you which side of that
line you're on, not default to the bigger ticket.

## What actually moves your price

- **Size.** Materials and labor scale with square footage — the per-square-foot
  ranges above are why a 3,800-square-foot roof costs so much more than a
  2,000-square-foot one.
- **Pitch and height.** Steep, tall roofs are slower and require more safety
  setup. The dramatic rooflines common in Carlton Woods and Sterling Ridge sit
  at the higher end of the labor scale.
- **Material.** The Modernize table above shows the spread within asphalt alone;
  step up to Class 4 impact-resistant shingles or standing seam and the bracket
  changes again.
- **Decking condition.** Rotted or delaminated sheathing has to be replaced
  before new shingles go on. Nobody can see all of it until tear-off, which is
  why a trustworthy estimate
  states the per-sheet decking price _in advance_ instead of surprising you
  mid-job. (The 2021 IRC also requires tear-off to the deck for roof
  replacement — recovering over a water-soaked deck, or where two or more
  layers already exist, isn't allowed.)
- **Complexity.** Valleys, dormers, skylights, chimneys, and multiple roof
  planes all add flashing work and cut waste.

## The Woodlands specifics: no county permit, but Township approval

Two local facts worth knowing:

1. **Unincorporated Montgomery County requires no permit for residential
   re-roofing** — that's straight from the county's own permitting FAQ. So
   there's no county permit fee in your project cost.
2. **The Township's approval is mandatory anyway.** The 2024 Residential
   Development Standards require prior written approval from your village's
   Residential Design Review Committee for all roof replacement, with colors
   muted and harmonizing with the landscape, and a review window of up to 45
   days. (Heads-up for Grogan's Forest Sections 5, 6 and 7: your applications go
   to the City of Shenandoah instead.)

We prepare and submit the RDRC application as part of every
[roof replacement in The Woodlands](/the-woodlands/roof-replacement) — it's how
you avoid a covenant headache after the fact.

## How to compare bids without getting burned

The Texas Department of Insurance's standing advice after storms is simple:
**get three bids.** We'd add: make sure they're bids for the same roof. A
number is only comparable if the estimate names the shingle line, the
underlayment, the warranty being registered, the nailing pattern, and the
per-sheet decking price. A bid that just says "30-yr architectural shingle —
$X" isn't cheaper; it's vaguer. And if a bid arrives thousands below the
published ranges above, ask what got left out — usually it's the tear-off, the
decking allowance, or the accessories that actually earn the manufacturer's
wind warranty.

## Paying for it

If a storm caused the damage, your homeowner's policy may cover replacement
minus your deductible — and under Texas law (Tex. Ins. Code §707.002), you must
actually pay that deductible; any roofer offering to "waive" or "absorb" it is
proposing something illegal. More on that process on our
[insurance claims page](/the-woodlands/insurance-claims). For age-related
replacement, ask us about payment options when we quote — everything goes in
the written estimate, nothing verbal.
