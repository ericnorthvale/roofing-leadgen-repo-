Ask three roofers in Montgomery County which architectural shingle to buy and
you'll hear TAMKO Titan XT and GAF Timberline HDZ more than anything else. Both
are premium laminated shingles, both are excellent, and both get oversold with
claims that don't match their own spec sheets. Here's the comparison straight
from the manufacturers' published documents — no embellishment.

## Construction and nailing zone

**Titan XT** is built with two fiberglass mats and a polyester reinforcing strip
in the common bond area — that strip is there to resist nail pull-through. Its
"Rapid Fire" nailing zone is a 1-1/2-inch expanded target backed by a reinforced
2-inch polyester nail line, with a polymer-modified sealant. Standard fastening
is four nails per shingle.

**Timberline HDZ** uses GAF's LayerLock technology, which mechanically joins the
shingle layers and powers the StrikeZone nailing area — GAF's spec sheet calls it
the industry's largest nailing zone — paired with Dura Grip sealant.

Both designs attack the same installer problem: a bigger, reinforced target
makes correct nail placement easier and more consistent. Call this one a wash,
with each company taking a different engineering route to the same goal.

## Wind warranties — the biggest real difference

**Titan XT:** a 15-year limited warranty for winds **up to 160 mph** — but only
when the shingles are applied per TAMKO's High Wind Application Instructions.
Install it the standard way and you don't get the 160-mph coverage, so make sure
your contract actually specifies the high-wind spec.

**Timberline HDZ:** the **WindProven limited wind warranty — 15 years with no
maximum wind speed limitation**. It has its own conditions: you need LayerLock
shingles plus four qualifying GAF accessory categories (starter strips, roof deck
protection, ridge cap, and either leak barrier or attic ventilation). Notably,
no contractor certification is required.

Both are strong. The practical difference is what triggers the coverage: TAMKO's
depends on the _installation method_, GAF's on the _components on the roof_. A
no-speed-limit warranty is also simpler to reason about after a storm — there's
no argument over whether the gust exceeded a number.

## Algae protection — a clear gap

This one matters more in the Gulf South than almost anywhere. ARMA identifies
_Gloeocapsa magma_ algae as the most prevalent cause of roof discoloration, worst
in the Gulf states.

- **Titan XT:** 10-year Algae Cleaning limited warranty.
- **Timberline HDZ:** 25-year StainGuard Plus algae-protection limited warranty
  (on packages bearing the StainGuard Plus logo).

Twenty-five years versus ten is the widest spread on this comparison sheet, and
it's not academic here: University of Georgia Extension notes that roofs in
humid areas often start darkening within 5–7 years, heaviest on shaded and
north-facing slopes. On a tree-covered lot — most of The Woodlands, in other
words — the streaks can show up before the first decade is out, which is
exactly when a 10-year warranty is expiring and a 25-year one is just getting
started.

## Impact resistance — both Class 3, full stop

Both shingles pass UL 2218 at **Class 3**. Not Class 4. If someone tells you
Titan XT is a Class 4 shingle, they're wrong — TAMKO's own data sheet says Class
3, and it even includes the disclaimer that UL 2218 steel-ball testing "may not
correlate with real world" hail. GAF is similarly candid: HDZ passes UL 2218 at
Class 3, may be eligible for insurance discounts, and hail damage is _not_
covered by the limited warranty.

If you want a true Class 4 shingle, that's a different product line — see
[our guide to Class 4 impact-resistant shingles](/blog/class-4-impact-resistant-shingles-texas).

## Lifetime terms

- **Titan XT:** limited lifetime warranty on single-family homes, with a 10-year
  "Full Start" non-prorated period up front.
- **Timberline HDZ:** lifetime limited warranty against manufacturing defects for
  the original owner of a single-family home.

Comparable structures. As with every shingle warranty, coverage steps down after
the initial non-prorated window, and the fine print is worth reading before you
sign — we'll walk you through either document line by line.

On the lab standards — D3161 Class F, D7158 Class H, UL 790 Class A — they're
identical. These are both top-shelf shingles by every published measure.

## So which one?

Honestly: you won't make a mistake with either. The decision usually comes down
to three factors.

1. **Algae exposure.** Heavily shaded lot, north-facing slopes, trees overhead?
   HDZ's 25-year StainGuard Plus is the stronger answer.
2. **How the wind warranty is earned.** Prefer a component-based system with no
   speed cap? HDZ with the WindProven package. Comfortable specifying TAMKO's
   high-wind installation in the contract? Titan XT's 160-mph coverage is
   excellent too.
3. **Color and availability.** In The Woodlands, your village's design review
   committee wants muted shades — both lines offer plenty that qualify, so it may
   come down to which palette you and the committee like, and what's in stock
   when your [roof replacement](/the-woodlands/roof-replacement) is scheduled.
